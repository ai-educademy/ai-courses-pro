## Why retrieval matters

Language models are excellent at transforming context, but they are not a source of truth. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) gives a model relevant, current evidence at request time.

## A production retrieval loop

1. **Ingest** documents with ownership, timestamps, permissions, and provenance.
2. **Chunk** by meaning rather than an arbitrary character count.
3. **Index** embeddings alongside searchable text and metadata.
4. **Retrieve** with hybrid search when exact terms and semantic similarity both matter.
5. **Rerank** the shortlist against the user’s question.
6. **Generate** an answer that cites the supplied evidence.
7. **Evaluate** retrieval and answer quality separately.

The important design choice is not the vector database. It is the contract between retrieval and generation: what evidence is allowed into context, how much, from which tenant, and what the model must do when evidence is missing.

## Failure modes to test

- The right document exists but was not retrieved.
- The retrieved passage is relevant but out of date.
- A user can retrieve a document they are not authorised to see.
- The answer sounds plausible but cannot be grounded in the supplied passages.

## Build challenge

Create a small knowledge assistant with five source documents. Add citations, a “not enough evidence” response, document freshness metadata, and a ten-question golden test set. Record retrieval recall and groundedness before changing the prompt.
